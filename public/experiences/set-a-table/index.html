THE WORK OF GATHERING

Make an evening your own.

Five short movements, about 10–15 minutes. Take your time, skip what doesn't fit, and come back to change anything. Your plan will take shape as you go.

01 / START WITH SOMETHING REAL

Think of a table you still remember.

Maybe the food was extraordinary. Maybe it was nothing fancy at all. Maybe an evening went somewhere nobody expected, and nobody was in a hurry to leave.

Before we plan a new gathering, let's start with one you've actually experienced.

What do you think made it stay with you? Choose any that fit.

FROM PETE'S TABLE

Some of my favorite evenings have been completely different from one another. I keep coming back to the details that make a particular night feel like it could only have happened that way.

There isn't a right answer here.

02 / GIVE IT A REASON TO EXIST

What brings these people together?

Some nights begin with a bottle you can't wait to open. Others start with a dish you've been working on, or simply people you want in the same room.

What do you want to put at the center of this one? You can choose a starting point and make it yours.

THE POINT IS YOURS TO CHOOSE

I've built some tables almost entirely around the wines. Others were big cooking experiments. Some were communal meals around a fire. I don't think one format has to stand in for all the others.

03 / GIVE IT YOUR TOUCH

What are you bringing to the table?

You don't need a six-course menu. But you probably have something you care about—a recipe, a favorite drink, a story, a small gesture—that could give this evening its own character.

How do you want the evening to feel? Choose a few.

A NOTE ON CRAFT

Sometimes the food is the whole adventure. It's okay to put real care into it—and it's just as okay if tonight's best idea is one good pot of something everyone can reach for.

04 / LEAVE ROOM FOR PEOPLE

Make a plan. Leave room for life.

The food and atmosphere matter. So do the little transitions: the first few minutes, who feels included, and whether your plan has enough flexibility to let the evening become its own thing.

Will you invite guests to help shape the evening?

ROOM TO STAY HUMAN

People don't have to agree, perform, or tell their deepest story for a gathering to matter. Sometimes the most generous thing is to give everyone a little room.

05 / TAKE IT TO THE TABLE

The rest happens when people arrive.

You've made some choices. That's enough to begin. Your Table Score is a working plan, not a script. Print it, keep it on your phone, and change it whenever you need to.

✳

Take your plan into the world.

Try it with real people. Notice what surprises you, what feels easy, and what you might do differently next time. That's where the learning really begins.
